What is a Magnitude Comparator?

A magnitude comparator is a combinational circuit that compares two binary numbers and produces three mutually exclusive outputs.

G — Greater
G=1 when A>B.
E — Equal
E=1 when A=B.
L — Less
L=1 when A<B.
Priority Rule
For multi-bit numbers, compare from MSB toward LSB. The first unequal bit determines the result.

K-Map and Simplification

G: 1 occurs for A=1,B=0 → G=AB̅.
L: 1 occurs for A=0,B=1 → L=A̅B.
E: 1 occurs for 00 and 11. These diagonal cells cannot form a K-map group → E=A̅B̅+AB=A XNOR B.

2-Bit Magnitude Comparator

Let A=A₁A₀ and B=B₁B₀. The MSB pair A₁,B₁ has priority. The LSB pair is considered only when the MSBs are equal.

X₁=A₁ XNOR B₁; X₀=A₀ XNOR B₀
G=A₁B̅₁+X₁A₀B̅₀
E=X₁X₀
L=A̅₁B₁+X₁A̅₀B₀

4-Bit Magnitude Comparator

Let A=A₃A₂A₁A₀ and B=B₃B₂B₁B₀. Comparison starts at bit 3. A lower bit is considered only if every higher bit pair is equal.

Xᵢ=Aᵢ XNOR Bᵢ;   E=X₃X₂X₁X₀
G=A₃B̅₃+X₃A₂B̅₂+X₃X₂A₁B̅₁+X₃X₂X₁A₀B̅₀
L=A̅₃B₃+X₃A̅₂B₂+X₃X₂A̅₁B₁+X₃X₂X₁A̅₀B₀

4-Bit Block / Logic Architecture

Each bit slice uses the exact 1-bit comparator principle: NOT + AND for greater/less and XNOR for equality. Higher-bit equality terms enable lower-bit decisions. A direct 4-bit truth table contains 256 rows, and direct K-map minimization would require an 8-variable K-map; therefore hierarchical Boolean simplification is the practical implementation method.
